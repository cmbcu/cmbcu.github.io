Some of our ongoing projects

Project 1: Evolution of microbial genomes

Motivation

Microbial molecular evolutionary process is a good system for computational modeling because it is fast and sensitive to diverse environmental factors

Their relatively small genome sizes also permit large-scale study with limited funding and computing resources

Plenty of public data for developing and testing new hypotheses

Research directions

Virulence factors in Leishmania parasites found in Thailand versus world-wide

Conservation of flaviviral UTR sequences and predicted structures across hosts

Project 2: Computational prediction tools for cancer immunotherapy

Motivation

Cancer cells produce unique molecular signals that can be exploited for treatment via the body's immune system

However, cancer cells also produce many other signals that drown out the useful ones

Computational prediction can narrow down promising molecular signals and prioritize those most likely to be therapeutic

Research directions

Discovery of cancer-specific neoantigens from transcriptomics and peptidomics data

Prediction of peptide-HLA binding, with focus on cross-allele knowledge transfer to address HLA alleles with small training data

Immunogenicity prediction for peptide-HLA complex using structural and biochemical embeddings

Project 3: AI-assisted chest radiograph reading

Motivation

Chest radiographs (CXR) is one of the first tasks in radiology that benefit from computer vision and deep learning

COVID-19 created a sharp demand for AI assistance to screen CXR images

Experience during COVID-19 helps us build a long-term partnership with radiologists

Research directions

Explainable CXR reading with accurate and precise lesion localization heatmaps

Radiologist workflow assistance via patient prioritization and automated report generation

Improving performance for serious lesions like pneumothorax

Project 4: Computer vision for cellular and tissue images

Motivation

Computer vision techniques enable quantitative assessment of cellular and tissue images

Heterogeneity of these images (cell types, background, staining, imaging specificiation, etc.) is a primary challenge

Large amount of detailed annotation is needed, but is not realistic to produce

Research directions

Multi-step annotation and model development workflows for cellular and tissue images

Digital pathology applications

Study of morphological changes during cellular and cancer development processes

Project 5: Single-cell multi-omics to understand stem cell, cancer, and immune systems

Motivation

Molecular labs in Thailand are transitioning from bulk to single-cell and spatial multi-omics

Bioinformatics tools have different assumptions and are appropriate for different experimental settings

Interpretation of such results requires a combination of biological knowledge and data science skills

Research directions

Collaborate with experimental scientists at MDCU to investigate molecular signatures in diverse diseases and biological systems

Examples include limbal stem cells, immune population post-infection, and cancer organoids

Project 6: Applications of data science and machine learning in biomedicine

Motivation

Data in biomedicine are difficult to analyze due to the complexity of the underlying mechanisms

A mix of hypothesis-driven and data-driven techniques is needed

Research directions

Collaborate with clinicians to make sense of their biomedical data

Examples include adverse effect of radiotherapy, association between mental stress and volatile compound in sweat, and impact of air pollution on cardiovascular disease
